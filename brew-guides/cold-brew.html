Cold brew concentrate, the way we make it at the bar

Dana Whitlock · June 12, 2026

Every summer we go through about forty gallons of cold brew a week at the café. Here is the same recipe scaled down to a quart jar.

Coffee
150 g, ground coarse (like raw sugar)
Water
750 g, cold, filtered
Ratio
1:5 concentrate
Time
16 hours in the fridge

Steps

  1. Grind the coffee coarse. Too fine and it will be muddy and hard to filter.
  2. Stir the grounds into the water in a large jar until there are no dry clumps. Cover it and put it in the fridge.
  3. After 16 hours, strain through a fine mesh sieve, then again through a paper coffee filter. Be patient with the second filter; it is slow.
  4. To serve, mix one part concentrate with one part cold water or milk over ice.

Which coffee to use

Chocolatey, lower-acid coffees make the most crowd-pleasing cold brew. Blue Ridge Breakfast and the Sumatra are our favorites. A natural Ethiopian like the Guji makes a fruity cold brew that people either love or find too wild.

How long it keeps

The concentrate keeps for about ten days in a sealed jar in the fridge, though it is at its best in the first week.
